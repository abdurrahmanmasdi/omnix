**OmniX: Istanbul market strategy, sellable MVP, and initial pricing**

26 September 2026. Product-manager recommendation based on public market research and a fresh source-level review. Founder context: first customers will come through cold outreach; the intended clinics serve international patients. Prices below were checked on public vendor pages today; vendor capabilities, customer counts, compliance statements, and performance claims were not independently audited. This document proposes a product direction and commercial experiments. It does not certify production readiness or change the application.

**Decision: sell one international dental-patient workflow first.** Keep the underlying messaging and AI infrastructure reusable, but focus the initial product, website, demo, onboarding, and sales effort on independent Istanbul dental clinics serving international patients. Add hair-transplant intake after paid use establishes a repeatable workflow. Building for almost every niche now would add different qualification rules, calendars, inventories, integrations, and sales objections before any acquisition channel is validated.

Dental is the initial recommendation because OmniX already has dental-specific prompts, documents, and intake routing, and the founder has no stronger warm distribution advantage in hair transplantation. It is not a claim that dental has a larger or more profitable reachable market. Interview hair clinics alongside dental prospects; a credible paid commitment can justify changing the first specialty before substantial new development.

| Choice | Benefit | Cost or uncertainty | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Almost every niche | Many possible buyers and opportunities to reuse messaging infrastructure. Some nonmedical uses have less sensitive data. | Each niche needs its own outcome, data, onboarding, and integrations. Cold outreach and reference customers become fragmented. General CRMs and local sector products are already alternatives. | Preserve technical flexibility; do not launch this commercial scope. |
| Dental and hair clinics together | Related international-patient communication, staff handoff, and consultation patterns. | Different approved questions, clinical-review materials, service packages, and demos. Shared healthcare obligations remain. | Sensible second step, not two simultaneous launches. |
| International dental clinics first | Closest fit to the present implementation; one buyer and one demonstrable workflow. | Crowded category; willingness to pay and acquisition cost unvalidated. | Start here and validate through actual commitments. |
| Hair-transplant clinics first | A focused consultation/intake use case, with possible reuse of shared infrastructure. | Requires adapting the dental flow; EVED and other specialized offers already target this exact segment. | Switch only if discovery produces stronger paid traction or easier delivery. |

**The first offer: OmniX International Patient Coordinator.** Proposed Turkish positioning: “Yurt dışından gelen WhatsApp taleplerini yanıtlayın, uygun hastaları konsültasyona yönlendirin ve her sonraki adımı takip edin.” English explanation: “Clinic-approved WhatsApp answers, qualified consultation requests, and an accountable next step for every international inquiry.”

The initial customer hypothesis is one clinic location, roughly 1–5 coordinators, recurring international WhatsApp inquiries, and a visible response or follow-up problem. Around 100–500 relevant monthly inquiries is a useful prospecting range to test; it is not a verified market norm. A lower-volume clinic can qualify if the workflow pain and economics justify it. Buyer: owner or international-patient sales manager. Daily user: coordinator. Clinical approval owner: the clinic's designated clinician.

Exclude initially: clinics with negligible relevant inquiries, buyers needing only a cheap local appointment bot, operations requiring a full hospital-system replacement, and requests for autonomous diagnosis or treatment selection. For clinics with a mature CRM, decide before signing whether a permitted integration or a lightweight human handoff is workable. Do not sell an unbuilt integration to obtain the contract.

**How to win the first customers is an execution hypothesis, not a claimed moat.**

| Proposed reason to buy | What OmniX must demonstrate |
| --- | --- |
| Small operational change | One number and a bounded inquiry cohort can be activated; clinic retains ownership/access; staff know how to take over; existing CRM/calendar can remain. Account-specific migration/coexistence must be verified. |
| Better coordinator follow-through | Every handoff has an owner, reason, deadline, and outcome. Overdue cases are visible. A sent notification alone is not success. |
| Trustworthy clinic-specific answers | Approved packages and policies, source/version traceability for staff, a correction process, and clinician control over personalized clinical decisions. |
| Useful local service | Turkish staff onboarding, clear commercial terms, reachable support in stated hours, and an onsite or video workflow review. Setup target: five business days after clinic inputs and provider permissions are ready; provider approvals are an external dependency. |
| Visible commercial value | Weekly inquiry-to-consultation report reconciled with clinic outcomes, with staff time and costs. Show observed results; do not label every after-hours message a recovered sale. |

These can beat an alternative for a particular clinic if delivered well. Several competitors advertise similar benefits. The durable advantage would have to come from reusable specialty knowledge, a consented evaluation set, smoother implementation, reliable integrations, references, and retention accumulated over time.

**Redesign around the coordinator's next action.** Preserve existing infrastructure and replace the generic CRM experience with five clear destinations:

| Screen | Main job and minimum contents |
| --- | --- |
| Today / Bugün | New handoffs, consultation requests awaiting confirmation, doctor reviews overdue, patient follow-ups due, and delivery/account issues. Each row shows owner, deadline, last action, and one clear next action. |
| Inbox / Gelen Kutusu | Conversation list, original transcript, AI/human status, patient language/timezone, and a right-hand patient summary. Actions: take over, assign, add internal note, request review, request/confirm consultation, and set follow-up. Staff translation is reviewable and preserves the original. |
| Patients / Hastalar | Funnel and patient record: interest, country, language, travel intention, assigned coordinator, latest approved quote, next action, and outcome timeline. Filter by overdue action and waiting party, not just hot/cold labels. |
| Clinic Knowledge / Klinik Bilgileri | Approved services, indicative prices/currencies, package inclusions/exclusions, clinician/clinic facts, logistics FAQ, and contact/availability rules. Draft → approved → retired content, with owner/date. Founder management is acceptable initially. |
| Results / Sonuçlar | Eligible inquiries, useful response times, consultation requested/confirmed/attended, quote/deposit outcomes, unresolved handoffs, and usage/cost. Show observation dates and missing outcomes. |

Move channel tokens, developer configuration, and infrequently used controls into setup/settings. Default staff language to Turkish with English available. Start patient support with the first clinic's two highest-volume languages and verify those languages; do not sell “every language.” A bilingual coordinator can cover translation during the first supervised pilot. Mobile web must support reading a handoff and taking action; a native app can wait.

**The complete MVP journey is inquiry → qualified interest → confirmed consultation → recorded outcome.** The doctor/quote stage may happen before or after the consultation according to clinic policy; do not force one universal clinical sequence. Keep consultation status and review status separate from commercial pipeline stage.

Example using synthetic data: an English-speaking prospect in London asks about implants and a hotel. OmniX answers using the clinic's approved package facts, captures interest and travel intention, and offers a consultation. The prospect can decline to upload a photo and still request a call. A coordinator receives the summary in their working language, owns the next action, and confirms an actual slot with both local timezones. The clinician supplies any individualized assessment/quote. Approved follow-up or a staff task runs at the appropriate time. The coordinator records attended, cancelled, missed, or lost. No step claims a confirmed booking before confirmation exists.

The MVP needs narrow records for: consultation (requested time, confirmed time, timezone, owner, status, external reference), next action (type, owner, due time, waiting party, completion), internal note (author/time), review (clinician, status, approved response/reference), approved quote (amount, currency, inclusions/reference, approval time, validity), and outcome events. Reuse existing lead/language/currency/source fields. Avoid a new generic workflow-builder framework. No automated treatment plan or clinical photo assessment is required for this launch.

One assisted contact means one unique WhatsApp contact receiving an AI response during that billing month, counted once even if they return. A reply turn means one AI answer to a patient message batch, regardless of how many display bubbles it is split into. Internal retries and failed deliveries do not consume customer allowance. This is OmniX's billing definition, not Meta's definition of a message.

The two allowances are independent; either may be reached first. Alert at 80% and 100%; require approval for a block. If no block is purchased, route further automation to visible staff handling while retaining inbox access. Never silently lose incoming leads. Meter both allowances before using them in contracts. Initial media handling can go to staff; do not sell unlimited audio/image analysis through this text-focused allowance.

Meta/BSP charges are separate, preferably paid directly by the clinic; disclose any provider fee and who bills it. No hidden markup or unlimited-messaging claim. International recipient markets and message categories affect cost. [Meta pricing](https://business.whatsapp.com/products/platform-pricing). Support is in clearly stated Istanbul business hours; automated answering hours do not mean a 24/7 human support commitment. Manual invoicing/payment collection is enough for the first customers.

The premium over cheaper clinic software is a hypothesis about supported automation and measured outcomes. If a prospect needs only a shared inbox or appointments, acknowledge the cheaper fit. Do not defend 9,900 TL by comparing feature counts or by multiplying all inquiries by treatment revenue. Test the proposal in real demos. Avoid a public three-tier menu until observed usage reveals meaningful segments.

**Validate price through decisions.** Use the same founding offer for the first three suitable prospects who commit; do not invent urgency or reference customers. On each loss, record the real reason: missing workflow, distrust, number migration, weak need, insufficient traffic, or price. After roughly ten qualified demos, if price is consistently the objection, test a smaller scope such as an after-hours intake-only offer before permanently discounting the full product. If no prospect accepts a paid pilot, one deliberately selected free design partnership is acceptable with live traffic, a named owner, agreed post-pilot price, and a firm end date. Do not accumulate open-ended free custom projects.

**First commercial cycle and decision gates.**

1. Discovery cycle: aim for ten dental workflow interviews and five hair-clinic comparison interviews. Prepare the narrow demo and gather paid-pilot commitments. If hair clinics produce materially stronger paid demand, revisit the specialty before implementing different clinical intake rules.
2. Build/activation cycle: complete the PM01–PM07 requirements applicable to the signed scope, review the data arrangements, evaluate supported languages, verify the live channel, and train the coordinator. Do not start the live-pilot clock while provider approval or clinic knowledge is missing.
3. Supervised pilot cycle: three clinics, 14 live days each, bounded genuine inquiry traffic, daily exception review, and weekly owner feedback. A suggested evaluation target is at least 30 eligible inquiries per clinic; below that, call the result inconclusive rather than claiming proven uplift. Extension is an explicit commercial decision.
4. Paid continuation: aim for at least two of the three to accept the agreed paid continuation. Track the same leads for 30–60 days for longer outcomes without promising 60 free days. Seek renewal, not just a first invoice.
5. Expansion gate: consider hair transplantation after five paying clinics, at least three first renewals, acceptable direct margins, and an onboarding pattern that reuses most of the workflow. This is a recommended internal gate, not a forecast. A second specialty should change intake content and approved knowledge more than the application architecture.

Evaluate inquiry-to-attended-consultation rate for comparable cohorts; separate new inquiries from existing-patient support, duplicates, and tests. Also track useful first-response median/P90, unresolved handoff age, confirmation time, no-shows, critical wrong answers, coordinator time, and direct cost. A reasonable proposed engineering target is P90 useful response within 60 seconds for the supported text flow, measured end-to-end; do not market it before verification. Any critical medical misstatement or unauthorized disclosure requires correction and reassessment before widening traffic.

A recent comparable baseline is useful, but changes in ads, language mix, staffing, or case mix can explain apparent lift. Early samples are directional, not a causal guarantee. Financial value should use incremental treatment contribution and actual labor saved; requested appointments and nominal treatment prices are not cash earned.

**Defer scope that does not help this first consultation outcome.** No full clinic replacement, hotel/flight/transfer booking engine, dental charting, autonomous medical-image interpretation, treatment recommendation, inventory, payment processing, voice-calling agent, native mobile app, universal workflow builder, or broad integration marketplace. Instagram moves earlier only when the first customer's relevant inquiry flow actually starts there. Existing-patient medical complaints should receive an appropriate clinic escalation, not a sales sequence.

Keep OmniX as the umbrella brand. Use one focused landing page and demonstration for international dental coordination. Show Turkish buyer copy, selected patient-language examples, explicit human involvement, pricing scope, real contact details, and substantiated trust statements. Remove unsupported SOC2 and hallucination-prevention assurances. Publish case studies only with customer permission and measured results.

The next implementation effort should begin with PM01–PM03: a patient can request a consultation without a photo, a coordinator owns and confirms it, and the outcome persists. That gives the rest of the product a concrete job and makes the first sales demonstration reviewable.
